# Lotería de los Pueblos — Game rules

## 1. Players and rooms

1. A game is played in a **room**. The person who creates it is the **organiser**; the room gets a code that is not in use by any other room.
2. Players join with the room code (typed, by link or by QR code), a name (1–24 characters; duplicates allowed) and a picture of their choice.
3. A round can start with **at least 2 players**. A room holds **at most 20 players**; a 21st player is offered a new room instead.
4. The organiser's screen is the **cantor's screen**: it calls the cards and shows the round. When creating the room, the organiser chooses whether that screen **only displays** the game or **also plays**: with "Jugar también", the organiser gets their own cartón and spelling choices on the same screen and plays by the same rules as everyone else (they count toward the 2-player minimum and their picks are scored in the same order as all others). The organiser can start the round, pause, resume, change the calling speed and end the round early.
5. A player who joins after the round has started gets a cartón with every card already called crossed off, and starts with 0 points.
6. A player who reloads or briefly disconnects gets back the same cartón, crossed squares, points and languages.

## 2. Languages

Each player chooses two languages, independently, and can change either at any time without losing their cartón, crossed squares or points:

1. **App language** — Español or English — for menus, buttons and messages.
2. **Spoken language** — Náhuatl, Maya yucateco, Quechua (Cusco-Collao), Español or English — the language the cantor speaks to that player, the language of their three spelling choices, and the language of the words revealed on their cartón.

Example: a Spanish speaker can keep the app in Español and hear Náhuatl to practise it.

All five spoken languages are always offered. Each player hears and reads the same card in their own spoken language at the same moment.

## 3. The cards

1. The deck has **54 cards** in six groups: animals, plants and food, sky, land, things made by hand, and places.
2. Every card has a picture and a name in each of the five languages.
3. Cards never show people, and the game never uses gendered roles for people.

## 4. The cartón

1. Each player gets one **cartón of 4 columns × 4 rows**: 16 different cards chosen at random from the deck, with at most 5 from the same group.
2. Cartones in the same room are kept different from each other (no two share more than 8 cards).
3. The cartón shows **pictures only**. A square's word, in the player's spoken language, appears once that card has been called.
4. The cartón is always visible during the round; nothing covers it.

## 5. Turns: calling a card

1. The cantor is the game's **automatic voice**, never a person. Once the round starts it calls cards on its own, one at a time, at the speed the organiser set (6 to 20 seconds per card).
2. **Each card is called once**, in a random order no player can predict.
3. When a card is called, every player **sees its picture** and **hears its name** in their own spoken language. The written name is not shown; players must recognise it among three spellings.
4. A player can replay the name as often as they like while the card is being called.

## 6. Picking the spelling

1. Every player gets **three written choices** in their spoken language: the called card's name and the names of two other cards. Everyone gets the same three cards in the same order.
2. Every player may pick, whether or not the card is on their cartón.
3. A wrong pick shows "¡Casi!" with no penalty, and the player may try once more while the card is being called.

## 7. Crossing off

1. **Everyone who has the called card on their cartón crosses it off**, whether they picked the right spelling, the wrong one, or none.
2. A crossed square shows a bean and the card's written name.

## 8. Scoring

1. For each called card, the **first player to pick the right spelling earns 2 points**.
2. **Every other player who picks the right spelling earns 1 point.**
3. A wrong pick, or no pick, earns 0 points.
4. Points belong to the current round only and are used **only to break ties**. There is no ranking across rounds.

## 9. Winning

1. The **first player to cross off all 16 squares** of their cartón completes it and calls **¡Lotería!**.
2. If **more than one player** completes their cartón on the same called card, **the one with the most points wins**. Players with the same points share the win.
3. Everyone then sees the ¡Lotería! screen with the completed cartón. "Otra ronda" starts a new round in the same room, with new cartones and points back at 0.
4. Players who did not complete see a friendly message, never a penalty.
5. Screens never use words for prizes, betting, money or winning (ganar, premio, apuesta, dinero, fichas, bingo, win, prize, bet, money, chips). The game says "¡Lotería!", "completaste" and "frijoles".

## 10. Audio

1. Every card's name is voiced in all five languages, from pre-recorded clips. The voices are generated with Meta MMS-TTS; the game says so and credits it.
2. A player hears only their own spoken language. A new call stops the previous one.
3. Players can turn the sound off on their device; the picture and choices still appear.
4. The cantor's screen plays no names unless the organiser turns its sound on.

## 11. Solo mode

1. One player plays against one or more **computer players** on the same device, without a network.
2. All rules above apply. The computer players pick after a short delay and are sometimes wrong, depending on the chosen level: Fácil, Normal or Difícil.

## 12. Classroom mode

Printed paper cartones are a separate classroom mode and are not part of the online game.
